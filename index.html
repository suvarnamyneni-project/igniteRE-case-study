01 Overview

The product and the role

FirstAm IgniteRE is described in the project document as a Gold Stevie Award–winning, mobile-friendly technology platform designed to enhance residential real estate transactions. It provides single sign-on access to tools and resources, so real estate professionals can securely share transaction details, access property data, create marketing materials, and take part in education.

My role was UX/UI design. The tools recorded for the work are Sketch, Figma, InVision, and Miro.

The home screen is the signature experience the later framework calls a useful dashboard. It keeps one shell around the work: primary navigation for Dashboard, Transactions, Properties, Marketing, Calculators, and Classes; a welcome; a sales rep; and a next step. Two states are drawn. A first visit offers ways to jump in. A return visit leads with messages and a task that is due.

02 Problem

Problem and project goals

A residential transaction already involves agents, lenders, attorneys, and the people buying or selling. The product has to make that work feel like one place: secure sharing of transaction details, property data, marketing materials, and classes, behind a single sign-on.

The project document sets five goals for the design.

Design system

Use systems thinking to arrive at repeatable UX patterns and a dynamic component library.

Global navigation

Design a unified navigation and UX shell grounded in the applicable user flows.

Quick win

Identify current customer-experience pain points where interaction and visual design can help. The file sets this goal and does not name which pain point was chosen.

Brand expression

Champion the new portal design as a showcase for an elevated First American visual brand experience.

Core capabilities

Craft interaction and visual design for the platform’s core capabilities.

Those goals are what the rest of the file answers. The framework and principles define the system. Personas and the transaction journey ground the navigation. The screens are the core capabilities. Icons, color, and the component sheets are the library.

04 Define

Foundations, intent, and principles

The foundations explain what the system is for. First American builds utilitarian tools to augment strong relationships with busy customers. The system holds guidelines, styles, and components so design can stay consistent and repeatable across existing and new experiences. Its purpose is to make design more collaborative, consistent, and streamlined, so attention can stay on user needs, shorter project timelines, and better Title and Escrow experiences.

Three principles follow. They are quoted from the project document.

1. Customers above all

Think about the needs of different types of First American users and be intentional about how to respond to them in a product or feature. Whenever possible, use research to inform your decision-making so you can map your process to how real users think and work.

2. Enable but don’t overwhelm

First American builds strong relationships with our customers around the services we provide. We also build powerful tools and systems meant to augment and streamline those relationships. Even though the systems we design are complicated, they should feel effortless. We accomplish this by removing unnecessary steps and making sure people understand what they need to do throughout the experience.

3. Unify and reuse

First American should present a cohesive experience to all users, no matter where they are in the product. Use consistent language, patterns, and components across the interface to make it easier for users to get stuff done.

The personas are the “customers above all” artifact: three roles, each with jobs, pains, gains, and the portal features that matter to them. The returning home screen follows the second principle in what it puts first — a secure message and a task that is due, before the rest of the portal. The third principle is the global navigation, the pattern library, and the component sheets.

06 Journeys

The transaction journey

One journey is documented: the transaction, from first contact through post-closing. It is a matrix, not a single happy path. Phases run across the top. Rows show who is involved, which channel carries the moment, and where the portal itself is used.

The roles are the buyer or seller, the real estate agent, the attorney, and the lender — the same kinds of people named in the personas, with the buyer or seller called out on the journey as their own row. Channels are mobile, web, in person, phone, email, mail, text, and fax. A “Utilizes Portal” marker flags phases that use the product. Under portal areas, Calculators is a short span near the start. Secure Portal runs across most of the transaction after that.

The map is the reason the shell cannot be only an agent tool. Phone, email, mail, and in-person contact stay in the journey beside the portal. The product is designed into specific phases, not in place of every channel.

08 Patterns

Ideation and UX patterns

Miro is one of the tools recorded for the project. Early boards and sketches are not in the file. What the file does include is the pattern set those explorations resolved into.

These patterns and guidelines create clarity and confidence for users so they can spend less time learning, and more time getting things done.

Eleven patterns are specified: navigation, panels, modals, tabs, expandable information, pop-overs, coachmarks, mobile patterns, cards, step-throughs, and breadcrumbs. They are the reusable answers to the design-system goal, and they are the pieces the key screens are built from. Tabs separate products, property views, classes, and transactions. Cards carry the dashboard and the class list. A step-through carries print customization. Pop-overs carry actions on a farm. The mobile pattern shows the shell collapsed to a menu.

09 UI

Key screens

The core capabilities are designed inside the same shell. Marketing, properties, classes, and transactions each keep the primary navigation, the SPOTLIGHT header, and the account. Sign-in and the invitation email are the way in. Screens below are shown at the sizes documented: web, tablet, or phone.

Marketing

Rachel’s card selects Marketing, and her jobs include sending marketing materials. On the web, print products live in the portal: a short argument for printed pieces, categories across the page, and a search over popular products. On the phone, the task narrows to two choices the screen makes explicit — a color, and how contact information appears on the front.

Properties

Property search and farming are jobs on Rachel’s card, and Properties is one of her selected features. The web screen is a map with search and recent lookups, under tabs for property search, farming, reports, and recorded documents. The tablet screen is a farm: a list of properties beside the map, and actions to run a combined report, export the farm, generate mailing labels, or remove a property.

Classes

Education is one of the four things the product overview promises. The web screen is a class detail: title, register, date and time, location, capacity, price, instructors, description, and a map. The tablet screen is the upcoming list, as cards, with date, place, price, and an attending state. Both match the class-details and upcoming-classes branches in the architecture.

Transactions

Lance and Angela select Secure Portal, and the journey keeps Secure Portal across most phases. On the web, open transactions are a table: who the parties are, the property, opened and estimated closing dates, the latest milestone, and activity. Closed transactions and an FAQ are tabs, and a new transaction can be started from the header. On the phone, one transaction fills the screen: overview, messages, and documents, plus a prompt for information that is still missing.

Sign in

The overview describes single sign-on. The sign-in screen asks for an Eagle ID to access SPOTLIGHT: username, password, a way to reset the password, and a way to request access. A photograph of a house fills the other half of the web layout, which is the brand expression goal applied to the front door.

Email template

The invitation email is part of the same entry. It welcomes the reader to SPOTLIGHT, explains the portal as a secure place to stay organized and close faster, and offers account setup. It names two things the portal can do — send messages and view transaction status — and introduces the sales rep. The template uses the eagle, the navy button, and the same sales-rep pattern as the product.

10 System

Design system

The design-system goal was repeatable patterns and a dynamic component library. The patterns are above. The library work in the file is the dashboard, named piece by piece, then iconography and color. Brand expression is specified here too: the eagle watermark, the SPOTLIGHT name in the header, and the palette.

Dashboard components

Five sheets annotate the same home screen so the parts can be reused. The welcome bar is the greeting plus widgets. The header is platform branding and utility navigation. The navigation bar is the eagle, primary navigation, and My Sales Rep. Jump In is the card area, with a card or list toggle. A further sheet calls out Help Center content, the help buttons, and the sales rep message.

Iconography

Primary navigation has its own six icons, one for each area of the shell: Dashboard, Transactions, Properties, Marketing, Calculators, and Classes. A second sheet is the working library: search, edit, filter, files, communication, navigation, and the rest of the actions the screens use. One drawing style covers both, so a phone menu and a web sidebar can share a symbol.

Color palette

Color is specified as a named palette with printed hex values. Navy and FA blue carry navigation and structure. Sunshine marks emphasis, such as a message that needs attention. Secondary and pastel colors separate cards and states without inventing a new palette per screen. The swatches below use the hex values printed on the sheet, and the sheet itself follows.

11 Responsive

Across web, tablet, and phone

The product overview calls IgniteRE mobile-friendly. The file shows that as designed screens, not as a written breakpoint spec. The same shell changes what it keeps on screen.

On the web, primary navigation stays open: Dashboard, Transactions, Properties, Marketing, Calculators, and Classes, with the eagle and the sales rep. Utility navigation — help, notifications, account — stays in the header. Tables and maps can sit side by side with their filters.

On tablet, the documented screens move the map and the list into one held surface. A farm keeps its properties and its map, and puts report, export, labels, and remove into a menu. Classes become a card grid. The invitation email is shown at this size too.

On the phone, the shell collapses. Marketing uses a menu button and a single column for one decision at a time. A transaction drops the table and shows one file, with overview, messages, and documents as tabs. The pattern library includes that mobile navigation as its own pattern, so the small screen belongs to the system.

13 Reflection

Learnings and reflection

What I can stand behind from this file is the through-line. A customer benefit — swift, easy business with First American — is tied to a shell, to golden paths, and to a component system. The screens stay inside that shell. Navigation, header, and sales rep are specified once and then used on marketing, properties, classes, and transactions.

Each persona card selects its own set of portal features, and the journey agrees. Rachel’s card selects Properties, Calculators, Marketing, and Secure Portal. Lance’s card and Angela’s card select Secure Portal. On the journey, Calculators is a short span near the start, and Secure Portal runs across most of the transaction, while phone, email, mail, and in-person contact continue beside the product. The shell keeps one mental model while the work inside it changes by role.

The five goals meet in the system. Global navigation is a map and then a bar. Brand expression is the eagle, the SPOTLIGHT name, and a named palette. Core capabilities reuse tabs, cards, tables, and step-throughs. The result the document records — more consistency, fewer redundancies, faster delivery — is what that kind of system is for.

A personal note on what I would change, and any research or test results behind the work, is not in the project file. That part is still open.
